# Exercise 39.4: notes on the solution

## 1. What the position explains

Very little. At 300 ps the estimate is 2.109 at the center, 2.084 at 30 mm and 2.022 at 55 mm, and
2.056 for the measurement's 15 regions together: the position accounts for 0.05 of a gap of 0.42
between 2.11 and the measured 1.69. At 500 ps the regions give 1.593 against 1.634 at the center,
and the measurement found 1.11. The chords through points within 55 mm of the center of a 100 mm
cylinder are long; the estimate only falls fast near the edge (1.74 at 99.9 mm). Chapter 39's
sentence about the regions was right in direction and, it turns out, small in size; the chapter now
says so, with these numbers.

## 2. The scale of the measure

The estimate compares the two reconstructions' noise at the localization's scale, about 45 mm at
300 ps, at convergence. The chapter's measures are at finer scales: regions 16 mm across for the
background variability, a filter of 8 mm for the filtered noise, one 2 mm pixel for the pixel
noise. Their gains are ordered by that scale: 1.69, 1.39, and below 1 for the pixel, which the
chapter measured noisier with time of flight. That ordering is what an estimate made for the
coarse scale leads you to expect, and it is an observation from three measures, not a derivation:
to know the gain at a scale, measure at that scale. A smaller lesion is judged at a finer scale,
so the gain that matters for it is smaller than the headline number.

## 3. What goes into a document

The measurement, with the conditions that make it reproducible: the object, the counts, the
iterations, the measure and its scale, the resolution and the machine. The estimate belongs next to
it as the expectation it was checked against, with the size of the disagreement and its reasons
stated as reasons, not as corrections applied to the number. A design that needs a gain at a given
lesion size measures it at that size.
