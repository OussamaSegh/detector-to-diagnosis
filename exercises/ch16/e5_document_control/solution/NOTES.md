# Exercise 16.5: notes

**A typo in an old history row.** The row describes a version that was approved and released,
and records point at it: a release record, a change request or an audit report says "version 2
of the SDP" and means the text that row describes. Editing the row changes what those records
seem to say, without anyone approving it, and a reader can no longer tell the corrected history
from a falsified one. The correction is a new version whose row says what was corrected. That is
what "control of documents" means in practice: every change is visible, and nothing that was
approved changes silently.

**What the checker cannot see.** It knows that a document changed, not whether the change was
reviewed and approved, or whether the new history row describes the change truthfully. Those
remain the reviewer's job; the checker only makes sure that there is something to review.
