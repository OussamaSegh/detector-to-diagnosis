# MiniDAQ 2.3.0 release record

The record of SDP section 8 for release 2.3.0, the last 2.x release: the latency judged against
the previous release's programs on the same machine (CR-0038, Theme L), illustrations and photos
in the book (CR-0039, Theme I), housekeeping from 2.2.0 (Theme H), the evidence without paths of
the machine (CAPA-0008), and a deviation for the latency criterion ACC-04 (MDQ-PR-096). Evidence paths
are relative to this directory's parent, `docs/regulatory/`; the files are in `measurements/release-2.3.0/` and were
written by `tools/release_checks.sh` unless the notes below say otherwise. Checked with
`tools/release_record.py check` (Exercise 14.5's `check()`) before the tag was made.

## Deviation

ACC-04 (SRS-PERF-022, the latency) failed: 8 of the 600 soak intervals had a 99th percentile
above 10 ms, where six are allowed. The criterion was set before these checks ran (CR-0035,
CR-0025, CR-0038) and was not changed after it failed; no check was run again.

The evidence that the failure is not in the commit is the comparison CR-0038 added for this
case: book-v2.2.0's programs, built from their tag and soaked on the same machine in the blocks
between the candidate's, had 10 slow intervals of 600 against the candidate's 8. Fisher's exact
test, one-sided, gives p = 0.7613 for the candidate being worse (ACC-10, alpha 0.05): no sign
that it is; the test had a power of 51% to find a candidate with twice the previous release's
rate. No class B source changed since 2.2.0, and the regression gate found every benchmark
equal to 2.2.0's.

What the evidence does not show: that SRS-PERF-022 is met. 8 of 600 is 1.33% (99% asked), and
the previous release's own programs, at 1.67% on this machine, fail the same criterion.

Safety: not relevant. No single was lost or late beyond the soak's checks, and the latency is no
risk control (MDQ-PR-096's evaluation, unchanged).

The software lead decided on 2026-11-26 (the coordinator, under the author's standing
delegation of 2026-11-15 and 2026-11-23, given in writing in the project) to release with this
deviation, as SDP version 25, section 8 provides, rather than hold and soak again. MDQ-PR-096
stays open; its closing (ACC-04 passing on a release soak, or a machine change that explains
the rate) is planned for 3.0.0, which closes every open item.

The rows of the table released with this deviation: `performance, SRS-PERF-022` and
`acceptance` (ACC-04).

Note of the review of 2.3.0 (2026-11-26): the decision stands, and the power above is too
high. Fisher's test treats the 600 intervals of each side as independent, but the slow ones
come in spells: five of the candidate's eight are in its first block (5, 0, 1, 2 and 0 by
block, a variance 2.7 times the mean), the previous release's are 2, 2, 3, 1 and 2. With the
block as the unit the samples are five pairs, and the candidate had more slow intervals than
the previous release beside it in two of them: no sign that it is worse, as the test says,
but a twice-as-slow candidate would pass more often than 49% of the time. The p-value and the
power stated next to ACC-10 hold only as far as the intervals are independent.
