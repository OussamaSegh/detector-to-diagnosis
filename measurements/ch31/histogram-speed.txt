Numba's first call, which compiles the function: 0.26 s
Every implementation gives the same counts and the same number outside, bin for bin.

## Why the C++ is slower than Numba: one experiment

The same solution source, built outside the project as a plain module (g++ 13 or clang++ 18, -O3,
no LTO), timed on the same 10,000,000 entries, minimum of 5 runs, in a separate session:

| build | time, ms |
|---|---|
| g++ -O3, the solution as it is | 32 to 34 |
| clang++ 18 -O3, the solution as it is | 28 |
| g++ -O3, the bin computed by a 32-bit division when the numbers fit | 26 |
| Numba, with lo, bin and bins passed as arguments (not constants) | 22 |

A 64-bit integer division is the slowest instruction in the loop; LLVM, which Numba and clang use,
tests for operands that fit in 32 bits and divides in 32 bits when they do. That explains 6 to 8
of the 10 to 12 ms between the g++ build and Numba; the rest was not found. (Corrected in the Part
VII review: this sentence first said "about a fifth", which these numbers do not support.) The C++ is not slower by design, but it is
not faster for being C++: the loop is the same loop, and the compiler decides.
