# Chapter 29: the C++ projector against the numpy projector

Machine: x86_64, 4 CPUs, Python 3.13.17, numpy 2.5.3. Module: build/release/recon.

## 1. Agreement

| Quantity | Largest difference, relative to the largest value |
|---|---:|
| forward, random image | 4.1e-08 |
| back, random sinogram | 4.9e-09 |
| back, 1 thread against 4 | 2.5e-15 |
| OSEM image, 4 iterations of 8 subsets | 1.0e-08 |

The numpy projector keeps its weights in float32 (about 7 significant digits); the C++ one computes them in double for every line.

## 2. Time of one projection (median of 7)

| Projector | Forward | Back |
|---|---:|---:|
| numpy | 57.1 ms | 48.9 ms |
| C++, 1 thread | 33.8 ms | 35.7 ms |
| C++, 2 threads | 20.8 ms | 27.0 ms |
| C++, 4 threads | 14.4 ms | 15.0 ms |

The numpy projector computes its weights once, in 0.50 s, and keeps them: 89 MB. The C++ one computes them for every line, every time, and keeps only the lines.

## 3. Time of a reconstruction: 4 iterations of 8 subsets

| Projector | Time |
|---|---:|
| numpy | 1.17 s |
| C++, 4 threads | 0.18 s |
