**Key points.**

- A chain is four different statements: the hazard, the sequence of events, the hazardous
  situation and the harm. A situation that repeats the hazard ("wrong event times") has not
  said who is exposed to what, so it cannot be rated.
- The rating is before risk control and takes the software's failure as certain. The probability
  comes from the sequence after the failure: how often its conditions occur, and whether anything
  in the clinic would catch the result.
- The rating must agree with the plan's criterion. The checker caught four of the model
  solution's own first ratings: S2 with P3 is acceptable by the sum rule, even though it felt
  like a risk to control. Acceptable risks are still reduced as far as possible.
- "Identification" for MiniDAQ means the wrong data behind a correct name (two runs mixed,
  simulated data stored as a scan), because MiniDAQ holds no patient identity.

**Common wrong answers.** Chains that start from a defect ("sorter bug") and end at the software
boundary; harms that are not harms to a person ("file corrupted"); every chain rated S1 because
"a physician will notice"; a probability for the software failure itself.

**Key points.**

- The invalidation looks at every counter that means loss and at the fault; one missing counter
  makes it a control with a hole that its unit test cannot reveal unless the test enumerates the
  counters.
- The alarm works on the loss of one interval, because a burst of loss late in a long run is a
  small fraction of the run's total. The fraction is of the packets sent, lost plus received.
- The alarm latches. An operator who looks away during the burst must still see it afterwards.
- Counters that go backward mean a new run; treating them as a huge loss would raise the alarm at
  every start and teach operators to ignore it.
- The invalidation is MiniDAQ's RC-01. The alarm's effectiveness depends on a person, so its
  evidence is a usability evaluation, not a unit test.

**Common wrong answers.** An alarm on the run's totals; an alarm that clears itself; a fraction of
the packets received rather than sent, which hides heavy loss; no handling of a new run; an
effectiveness column that repeats the unit tests.

## Exercise 15.3: An FMEA of the coincidence sorter


**Key points.**

- The rows come from the sorter's functions in its order of operations, plus the ones around it:
  closing windows at the end of a run, the accounting, the configuration.
- Every comparison is checked one unit either side, every loop with zero, one, two and many
  singles, every choice between candidates for its tie rule. The tie rule of
  `take_winner_of_goods` is the gap the code reading finds: implemented, commented, and
  required by nothing.
- Each effect names the hazard it can lead to, so the FMEA feeds the hazard file rather than
  standing beside it.
- An undetected failure is not allowed to end with "none": it needs a new control, proposed as a
  requirement.

**Common wrong answers.** Rows that restate the requirements as failures ("does not meet
SRS-SRT-001") without a cause; an occurrence column filled with guesses and multiplied into a
risk priority number; no row for the end of the run, which is where the only uncounted loss of
the sorter hides.

## Exercise 15.4: The minimal cut sets of the data-loss fault tree

```{.python include="exercises/ch15/e4_fault_tree/solution/cutsets.py" snippet="cutsets"}
```

**Key points.**

- OR gives the union of its inputs' cut sets; AND gives one set from each input, joined.
  Frozensets make repeated events count once and duplicates disappear.
- Minimization removes every set that contains a smaller cut set. Without it, the list grows with
  every repeated event and hides the short sets that matter.
- As drawn, the data-loss tree has fifteen cut sets of order two. Drawn with the dependency that E5
  (open windows not closed) also causes E6 (a loss without a counter), E5 alone is a cut set: the
  verdict cannot detect what was never counted, so the control must prevent it (SRS-PIPE-012).

**Common wrong answers.** Lists instead of sets, so `("A", "A")` survives; minimizing before the
expansion is complete; treating every non-OR gate as AND; a recursion without cycle detection.
