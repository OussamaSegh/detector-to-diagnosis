## Exercise 13.1: The standards map

The solution's edges:


**Key points.**

- IEC 62304 requires a quality management system and names ISO 13485 only as one way to have
  one, in a note. The arrow is "references": a team without a 13485 certificate can still
  conform, but must show that its own system covers document and change control, records,
  reviews and problem handling.
- IEC 62304 *requires* ISO 14971: its risk management process must comply with it. A software
  team cannot conform to IEC 62304 without the risk file.
- IEC 60601-1 requires ISO 14971 for the whole electrical system and, for programmable systems,
  makes the software life cycle of IEC 62304 apply. IEC 62366-1 requires ISO 14971 too.
- ISO 13485 references ISO 14971 for risk management in product realization, but does not
  depend on IEC 62304; ISO 14971 depends on neither.
- The MDR names no standard. A harmonised standard gives presumption of conformity because the
  Commission published its reference against the regulation; only the regulation's side of the
  map has "presumption" arrows.
- Editions belong in the labels, because the arrows can change with them.

**Common wrong answers.** An arrow from ISO 13485 to IEC 62304 ("the QMS requires the software
standard"); "MDR requires IEC 62304"; one unlabeled arrow type for everything; no editions.

## Exercise 13.2: What a software change triggers

**Key points.**

- The source code is unchanged, but the shipped program is not: a new compiler, new libraries
  and a new kernel make new binaries with new timing. IEC 62304 treats the platform as SOUP.
- The hazards are specific to a DAQ: packet loss from a new driver or a smaller default
  receive buffer, latency spikes from a changed scheduler, different behavior at power loss.
  Chapters 4, 9 and 12 built the tests for them, so verification is the system and
  performance regression, plus a phantom scan compared with released results, not the unit
  tests alone.
- The safety class does not change by itself, but a segregation argument that relied on the old
  platform must be checked again.
- EU: the notified body's change procedure decides whether approval is needed before release;
  the technical documentation is updated in any case. US: FDA's 2017 guidance on software
  changes, with a documented decision. Switzerland: the change follows the CE marking.
- The engineer prepares the analysis; regulatory affairs and the change control board decide.

**Common wrong answers.** "No regulatory impact, the code did not change"; "the unit tests
pass"; deciding alone whether the notified body must be told; forgetting the SOUP list and the
SBOM.

**Key points.**

- Three EU clocks, in calendar days from awareness: 2 days for a serious public health threat,
  10 for a death or an unanticipated serious deterioration, 15 for any other serious incident.
- The US five-day report counts work days; weekends and federal holidays are skipped, and which
  days are holidays is input, not code.
- The missing-quarter case: a possible serious deterioration, so a potential serious incident
  even without known harm. EU report by 22 January 2027 (15 days from 7 January); US 30-day
  report by 6 February, or a five-day report by Thursday 14 January if a field action is needed.
- An initial report can precede the complete one; waiting for the root cause is not a reason to
  miss the deadline.
- The PSUR is due at least every two years for class IIa and yearly for IIb and III.

**Common wrong answers.** One deadline for every incident; counting five calendar days;
starting the clock when the investigation confirms the cause; "no harm, so not serious".

## Check yourself: model answers

1. A QMS standard (ISO 13485) describes the organization: document and change control, training,
   purchasing, design control, complaints, CAPA. A life-cycle standard (IEC 62304) describes the
   processes for one kind of product. For a sorter change, ISO 13485 asks that the change is
   reviewed, verified, approved and recorded under change control; IEC 62304 asks which software
   items change, which requirements and risk controls are affected, which regression tests run,
   and that the change goes through problem resolution and configuration management.
2. The manufacturer, from the risk analysis: each software system and item gets class A, B or C
   by the harm its failure could contribute to, after considering risk controls outside the
   software. The MDR class is the device's, from Annex VIII and the intended purpose. A class IIa
   scanner can contain class C software items.
3. Rule 10: an active device imaging the distribution of radiopharmaceuticals is IIa, one that
   emits ionizing radiation for diagnostic radiology is IIb; a PET/CT is both, and implementing
   rule 3.5 takes the stricter. The DAQ software is IIa in the first and IIb in the second, by
   implementing rule 3.3.
4. Presumption of conformity with the requirements the standard covers. Non-harmonised standards
   are still the state of the art that Annex I requires, and notified bodies and FDA expect them;
   a manufacturer that does not use them must show another way that is at least as good.
5. An independent organization designated by a member state to assess conformity. For class IIa
   it audits the QMS, assesses the technical documentation of representative devices and runs
   surveillance audits. It must approve changes to the approved device that could affect safety
   and performance or the conditions of use before they are implemented.
6. All three are ways to get FDA's permission to market a device; the device type's class, and
   whether a predicate exists, decide which applies. A PET scanner is class II under 21 CFR
   892.1200 and uses a 510(k).
7. Whether a failure or flaw of any software function could present a hazardous situation with a
   probable risk of death or serious injury before risk controls: then Enhanced, otherwise Basic.
   The level changes how much documentation goes into the submission, not how the software is
   developed.
8. Since the MDR began to apply in 2021 without an updated MRA, the EU treats Switzerland as a
   third country for medical devices, and a manufacturer outside the EU needs an EU authorized
   representative (Article 11). An updated MRA would restore mutual recognition of conformity
   assessments, so the Swiss manufacturer would be treated like an EU one again; it was signed in
   2026 and is not expected to apply before late 2027.
9. It must be assessed as a potential serious incident: the definition includes incidents that
   might have led to a serious deterioration, such as a missed diagnosis. With no death and no
   known serious deterioration the 15-day deadline applies, from the day the manufacturer became
   aware, not from the end of the investigation.
10. The run record (configuration and the counters of the link, the sorter and the writer), the
    list-mode file's header and its integrity check, the audit trail of control commands and the
    service's log around the run. They must be recorded at the time because nothing afterwards
    can reconstruct which stage lost the data or which settings were in force.
