## Exercise 27.1: Crystal efficiencies from a normalization scan

```{.python include="exercises/ch27/e1_normalization/solution/norm.py"}
```

**Key points.**

- A fan sum is a crystal's efficiency times the sum of its partners' efficiencies along the lines
  that the source fills: `F[i] = eff[i] * sum_j eff[j] expected[i, j]`. Divide by that sum, not
  by the mean fan sum, and iterate, because the sum contains the unknowns.
- Damp the iteration with the geometric mean of the old and the new value. Undamped, the
  estimate swings between too high and too low on a scanner where each crystal has few partners.
- Fix the overall scale at mean 1 at the end. A normalization only fixes the crystals relative to
  each other; the absolute scale comes from a calibration against a known activity.
- A line's efficiency is the product of its crystals', because both photons must be detected.
- A dead crystal is a decision, not a division: its lines measured nothing, so set them to 0 and
  name the crystal, so that the reconstruction can leave them out.

**Common wrong answers.** Dividing each fan sum by the mean fan sum, which is 21% wrong rms for
the cylinder off center and still above the limit when centered, because each crystal of the
small scanner has only about 100 partners. Normalizing a line by the mean of its crystals'
efficiencies, which leaves half of each crystal's deviation in the data. Dividing by a dead
crystal's efficiency of 0.

## Exercise 27.2: The randoms of every line

```{.python include="exercises/ch27/e2_randoms/solution/randoms.py"}
```

**Key points.**

- A random pair needs the second single within the window either way round, a window of 2 tau:
  `R_ij = 2 tau r_i r_j` per second for the rates `r`, times the run's length for counts.
- The randoms of a line factorize, `R_ij = x_i x_j`, so each crystal's fan sum of delayeds,
  `D_i = x_i * sum over its fan of x_j`, determines `x`. Solve it by a damped fixed point and
  return `x_i x_j`: each line's estimate now rests on two fan sums of 69,000 counts instead of its
  own 711, and its noise falls by a factor of 7.
- The estimate keeps every crystal's fan sum exactly. Its error per crystal is therefore the
  Poisson error of that fan sum, and the test counts it in standard deviations of that error.

**Common wrong answers.** Counts used as rates with one tau, as the starter does, which is
`seconds / 2` times too large: 30 times for the test's run of 60 s. The
Casey formula `D_i D_j / sum(D)` on a scanner whose fan is narrower than the ring: it assumes
every crystal meets every other, and gives half the randoms for a fan of half the ring and a
sixth for a fan of a sixth. Subtracting the raw delayeds per bin, which is unbiased and puts the
whole Poisson noise of the delayeds into the data.

**Key points.**

- Move the line into the ellipse's frame: subtract the center's projection on the normal from s,
  and turn the normal by minus the ellipse's angle. The ellipse reaches
  `h = sqrt(a^2 n_x^2 + b^2 n_y^2)` along that normal, and the chord at distance d is
  `2 a b sqrt(h^2 - d^2) / h^2`. A circle is the case a = b, which is the first test.
- Keep units in the names: `mu_per_cm` and `chord_mm`. The factor 10 between them is the bug
  that the known value `exp(1.92)` for 200 mm of water catches.
- The decay factor of a frame is `exp(lambda t0)` times `lambda T / (1 - exp(-lambda T))`: the
  activity at the reference time over the mean activity during the frame. Use `expm1`, so that a
  frame much shorter than the half-life does not lose its digits.

**Common wrong answers.** A circle of the mean radius, whose factors are between 0.51 and 1.85
times the ellipse's for the head of the exercise. The decay corrected to the start of the frame
only, 3.1% low for a frame of 10 minutes at 60 minutes with fluorine-18. A frame correction that
uses the middle of the frame and is right only while the frame is short against the half-life.
