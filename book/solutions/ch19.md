## Exercise 19.1: Account for every packet

```{.python include="exercises/ch19/e1_link_accounting/solution/accounting.py" snippet="check"}
```

**Key points.**

- Every identity rests on one unreported number, the delayed packets that came too late:
  `delayed - both - reordered`, where `both` are the delayed packets whose copy went on time.
  Their sequence numbers are in `packets_lost`, the packets themselves in `packets_stale`. Once
  you have that number, the lost and stale counts are exact, not "about".
- `reordered <= delayed` is a check in its own right: a packet that the sender did not delay
  cannot come back out of order on loopback, so a larger count means the tracker reorders
  something it should not.
- `words` holds only when nothing was lost: the sender does not report how many singles were in
  the packets it dropped. Check what you can know exactly, and say what you cannot.
- The recordings must contain the case each identity is about. A damaged run without late
  packets would let a check that ignores them pass, which is why the heavy recording is there.

**Common wrong answers.** `lost == sent["lost"]`, which fails as soon as one delayed packet
comes too late; comparing `packets_received` with `datagrams` and forgetting rejected
datagrams; tolerances ("within 1%") where the counts are exact.

## Exercise 19.2: What the golden comparison can see

```{.python include="exercises/ch19/e2_golden_power/solution/power.py" snippet="formula"}
```

**Key points.**

- The starter's `1 + z_a sqrt(2/n)` is the difference found half of the time (power 0.5), with the
  variance taken at `r = 1`. Power enters through `z_b`, and the variance grows with `r`.
- The simulation checks the formula, and the formula checks the simulation: when they disagree,
  one of them is wrong, and the calibration test at no difference says which.
- For the golden run of seed 1 (229,415 prompts, 2,235 delayed), the prompt comparison finds a
  change of 1.35% and the delayed comparison one of 14.15%, at `0.001` and 90% power. A real 10%
  change in the randoms rate passes more often than it fails.
- The lever is the count, not the threshold: the detectable difference falls as `1/sqrt(n)`. A
  5% change needs about eight times the delayed counts, from a longer golden run or a
  configuration that makes more randoms.

**Common wrong answers.** Raising the significance level until the delayed comparison "works",
which makes every comparison fail correct builds more often; computing power with the exact test
inside a loop of millions of counts (it is slow and not needed: the normal approximation is
excellent there); a simulation with one trial per ratio.

## Exercise 19.3: Reading a soak test

```{.python include="exercises/ch19/e3_soak_analysis/solution/soak_analysis.py" snippet="trend"}
```

**Key points.**

- A trend counts when `b - 2 se` exceeds the limit. Without the margin, a flat run with half a
  MiB of noise per sample exceeds 1 MiB/h by chance in about one run in 60; with it, noise alone
  essentially never does.
- The warm-up is part of the method: pools fill and pages are touched in the first minutes, and a
  slope through them reports the start-up, not a leak.
- Counters are checked sample to sample: a decrease means a wrap or a reset, and every rate
  computed across it is wrong.
- The stall check compares each interval with the median interval of the same run, so it needs no
  configuration and finds a pause even when the average rate looks fine.
- A single 2 MiB allocation at 15 minutes gives a slope of about 6.7 MiB/h over the window and
  is reported as `memory`. A straight line cannot tell a step from a ramp; fit the two halves
  separately, run longer, or read the allocator's own counters.

**Common wrong answers.** Comparing the last sample with the first; a fixed tolerance on the
slope with no standard error; including the warm-up; checking the rate against the configured
rate only (it misses a stall in a source that was slow from the start, and fails a run whose
source is slightly slower than configured).

## Exercise 19.4: The test that finds an integration defect

```{.cpp include="exercises/ch19/e4_batching_test/solution/test_batching.cpp" snippet="metamorphic"}
```

**Key points.**

- The specification says the result is the same for every batch size. That relation needs no
  expected answer: the run with one batch is the oracle for every other batching. It catches the
  four mutants that live at batch boundaries (ties ordered only within a batch, pairs made at the
  end of a batch, a dropped short batch, a repeated boundary single).
- Mutant 5, the last candidate instead of the first, is the same in every batching. Only a worked
  example with two candidates in one window, computed from the specification, catches it. A
  metamorphic relation and an oracle are complements, not alternatives.
- Mutant 1 needs ties in the stream. With time steps that are never zero, the relation passes it;
  the generator forces a tie in 30% of the steps.
- Batch sizes 1 to 17 put boundaries everywhere, and sizes that do not divide the length leave a
  short last batch (mutant 3).

**Common wrong answers.** Comparing batch size 1 with batch size 2 only; streams with no ties;
reading the mutants and testing them one by one instead of the specification; a test of
`Pairer` alone with one batch, which is the unit test that already passed.

## Exercise 19.5: Record a readout link and replay it

```{.python include="exercises/ch19/e5_udp_replay/solution/udp_replay.py" snippet="replay"}
```

**Key points.**

- The replayer waits for each datagram's deadline from the start, not for each gap, so timing
  errors do not add up over a long capture.
- The recorder raises its receive buffer, and the test checks that it holds exactly the datagrams
  the sender put on the wire. A recording that lost datagrams would replay a different stream.
- Two replays give the same run only when the server's decisions depend on order, not on time.
  The sequence tracker gives up a gap after `max_hold_us`, a time; the test sets it to one second
  so that the reorder window decides. With the default of 1 ms, a delayed packet near the limit
  can be used in one replay and too late in the next.
- Not every counter describes the data: `receive_batches` counts the server's reads of its socket
  and depends on scheduling, so the comparison leaves it out.
- A replay compares runs with each other. It reproduces what the DAQ received, not what the link
  did: a datagram lost before the capture point is not in the capture.

**Common wrong answers.** Sleeping for each recorded gap (the drift grows with the capture);
timing with `time.time()`, which jumps with the wall clock; replaying at speed 0 into the server,
which can overrun its receive buffer; comparing a replay's counters with the original run's.
