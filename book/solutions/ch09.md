**Key points.**

- Extract coarse and fine together as one 50-bit code in 5 ps units: the coarse tick is
  exactly 1,024 fine bins, so `code * 5` is the time in ps and nothing else needs scaling.
  The largest time, 2^50^ × 5 ps, is 5.6 × 10^15^ ps and fits easily in 64 bits.
- The half-range rule: keep the largest code seen. A new code more than 2^49^ below it is a
  wrap; more than 2^49^ above it is a late word from before the latest wrap (give it the
  previous epoch); anything closer is ordinary disorder.
- The starter's "a step back is a wrap" adds 94 minutes to every time stamp after the first
  late word. Nothing crashes; every coincidence with that channel is lost from then on.
- Check the type field first: a status word decoded as a hit is a hit at a random time on a
  random channel, and nothing downstream can tell.

**Common wrong answers.** Adding the fine code to the coarse count without scaling;
comparing only the coarse part (late words within one tick are then mishandled); keeping
`last` as the most recent code instead of the largest, so that one late word moves the
reference backwards; testing the wrap by running for 94 minutes instead of starting near it.

## Exercise 9.2: Counting loss and reordering on a link

```{.cpp include="exercises/ch09/e2_link_monitor/solution/link_monitor.cpp" snippet="monitor"}
```

**Key points.**

- `int16_t(uint16_t(seq - highest))` is the signed distance modulo 2^16^. The starter's
  unsigned gap made a packet one behind look like 65,534 lost packets.
- A monitor that holds nothing must count a gap as lost when it appears and take it back
  when the packet turns up (lost − 1, reordered + 1). Its counts are then exact at the end and
  correct at any moment up to packets still in flight.
- A bitmap of the last 1,024 sequence numbers tells a late packet (bit clear) from a
  duplicate (bit set). When the highest number advances, the bits of the numbers it skipped
  must be cleared, or a slot's old meaning survives one trip around the window.
- With the fault injector's delays of at most 8 positions and no duplicates, every delayed
  packet is reordered once and every dropped packet is lost: the test demands equality.

**Common wrong answers.** Counting a late packet both as lost and as reordered; treating
every packet behind the highest as a duplicate; a window smaller than the largest
reordering; forgetting that the 16-bit counter wraps every 1.2 seconds at 55,000 packets per
second.

**Key points.**

- Between sync pulses k and k + 1, local time maps linearly to global time:
  global = (k + (l − L~k~) / (L~k+1~ − L~k~)) · T. That removes offset, drift and slow changes
  of the drift, to within the sync stamps' jitter.
- With exact stamps the residuals are within 5 ps (rounding to whole picoseconds); with
  100 ps jitter per photon the corrected differences have the photons' own spread,
  100 · √2 = 141 ps.
- The drift from the data alone is the slope of t~B~ − t~A~ against t~A~: −19.9998 ppm for
  clocks at +10 and −10 ppm. Its statistical error after 40,000 pairs is about 10^−6^ ppm.
- In a real scanner the boards share a reference clock, and this estimate becomes a
  monitoring check: a coincidence time peak that moves means synchronization was lost.

**Common wrong answers.** Correcting only the offset (the starter: the error grows by 20 µs
per second); one global linear fit through all sync stamps, which fails as soon as the drift
changes with temperature; extrapolating from the first two stamps for the whole run;
computing the slope in raw picoseconds without centering, which loses precision in the sums.

**Key points.**

- At a threshold of 2% of the 511 keV amplitude, the leading-edge crossing walks by 44 ps at
  100 keV, 16 ps at 200 keV and 5 ps at 350 keV relative to 511 keV, and by −2 ps at 650 keV.
- A constant fraction of each pulse's own peak removes the walk for a fixed pulse shape: in
  this model the crossing at 10% is the same at every energy to well below 0.01 ps.
- On the exponential tail the pulse falls below a threshold at about τ~decay~ · ln(A / thr),
  so ToT grows by τ~decay~ · ln 2 = 27.7 ns per doubling of energy: logarithmic, compressed at
  high energy, and in need of a calibration curve.
- The starter's 50 ps scan quantized the crossing to 50 ps, coarser than most of the walk it
  was meant to measure.

**Common wrong answers.** Measuring walk with a step coarser than the walk; a constant
fraction of a fixed reference amplitude (which is a fixed threshold again); reading a ToT
value as if it were proportional to energy.

**Key points.**

- A frame occupies payload + 66 bytes of wire time: UDP 8, IPv4 20, Ethernet header 14,
  frame check sequence 4, preamble and start-of-frame delimiter 8, inter-frame gap 12; frames
  shorter than 64 bytes are padded.
- 182 words per packet: 1,538 wire bytes per 182 singles, 8.45 bytes per single, 14.8 M

  second.
- 32 words per packet: 10.56 bytes per single, 11.8 M singles/s per Gigabit link, and 5.7
  times the packet rate. The packet rate is a separate budget: the receiving CPU pays per
  packet.

**Common wrong answers.** Counting only payload, IP and UDP (the starter: 38 bytes per frame

## Check yourself: model answers

1. **A board drifting by 1 ns per second.** Its time stamps move relative to every other
   board by 1 ns each second, so after a few seconds (two to four for a window of a few
   nanoseconds) its true coincidences with other boards fall outside the window and are lost,
   while randoms are unaffected: trues fall, randoms
   stay, and the image loses those lines of response. From the data alone: histogram t~B~ − t~A~
   for pairs between that board and the others per one-second slice; the coincidence peak
   moves by 1 ns per second, far more than its own width.
2. **A 94-minute wrap.** A bug in rollover handling appears only in runs longer than 94
   minutes, so it survives every short test and shows up in a long clinical or calibration
   run. A counter that wraps every second exercises its rollover code continuously.
3. **The half-range rule.** Two codes less than half the range apart are close in time in
   whichever order they come. A step back of more than half the range is a wrap; a step
   forward of more than half the range is a late word from before the latest wrap. "A step
   back is a wrap" turns every late word into a full wrap period added to all later times.
4. **Walk.** A smaller pulse rises more slowly and crosses a fixed threshold later, so the
   time stamp depends on the deposited energy. Remedies: a per-energy walk correction in
   software, constant-fraction timing, or a very low threshold on a steep leading edge.
5. **ToT.** On the exponential tail, the time to fall below a threshold grows with the
   logarithm of the amplitude, so equal steps in energy give shrinking steps in ToT. An
   energy window on raw ToT values is not a window in keV; ToT must be calibrated per channel
   with a nonlinear curve first.
6. **UDP rather than TCP.** A detector cannot wait for retransmissions, and an FPGA cannot
   afford to buffer unacknowledged data; TCP would move loss into the detector's buffers,
   where nobody counts it. In exchange, the receiver must make loss visible: sequence
   numbers, counters for every outcome, and a policy for runs with loss.
7. **Packet 2 after the gap was given up.** It is stale: counted and dropped. Delivering it
   would put its words, which are earlier in time than packets 3 to 5 already delivered, out
   of order, and the sort stage would fault the run.
8. **10 M singles/s on Gigabit Ethernet.** 67.6% full with 182-word packets, at 54,945 packets
   per second. With 32-word packets, 84.5% full (11.8 M singles/s is the limit) at 312,500
   packets per second: little margin in bytes, and 5.7 times the CPU cost per packet.
9. **"8 MiB granted".** Linux doubles the stored value and reports the doubled value, so the
   data buffer is 4 MiB. Compare the granted value with twice the request, and read
   `net.core.rmem_max`: the request is capped there without an error. MiniDAQ reports the
   halved value and warns.
10. **What sequence numbers do not reveal.** Loss at the end of a stream: nothing follows the
    gap. Close it with an end-of-stream marker that is acknowledged, or with periodic packets
    that carry the sender's running count of packets sent.
11. **Before the first data word.** The length is at least the header; the magic; the version;
    undefined flag bits are zero; the word count is in range; the datagram length equals
    header plus word count times word size; reserved fields are zero. Only then read the
    payload, with explicit byte order.
